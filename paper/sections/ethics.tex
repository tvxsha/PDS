\section{Ethics and Responsible Release}
\label{sec:ethics}

\paragraph{Dual use.}
A study of how RAG-poisoning detectors fail is also a partial guide to evading
them. Our red-teaming surfaced one evasion, boilerplate anchoring: a malicious
clause prefixed with generic licence text so that the embedding score drops
(Section~\ref{sec:devset}). We report it with the pattern that closes it and the
limits of that fix (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), because withholding a blind
spot in a published class of defences serves practitioners worse than disclosing it
alongside what is known about it.

\paragraph{Data provenance.}
Clean and poisoned documents come from public benchmarks (BIPIA and PoisonedRAG,
with passages from Natural Questions) and from public open-source READMEs.
The sources of the READMEs are logged in \texttt{data/\_provenance/} and the
licences of the benchmarks in \texttt{docs/contamination.md}. We use the benchmark
texts for research only. The public repository does not contain them or the pooled
document file; both are rebuilt from the original sources. It does contain short
README excerpts, each with its source project logged, which remain under the
licences of those projects. The poisoned, adversarial and cross-domain in-house
documents in the repository were written by the team for this evaluation and do
not reproduce a real attack or incident. We added no private or user data; we did
not screen the public texts for personal information.

\paragraph{Scope of claims.}
This work does not claim a new detection principle. The signals are existing ones;
the sentence classifier is a standard linear text classifier fitted on our
training role, and the fusion is a logistic regression. The contribution is the
comparison of these signals under one protocol, where they fail, and what a
cheaper cascade costs in recall.

\paragraph{Release.}
The code, the in-house documents, the per-document scores and the lock files of
the held-out runs are public at \url{https://github.com/tvxsha/pds}. The code that
produced the reported runs is tagged \texttt{freeze-v1}. The evasion pattern
described above and its fix are part of the released detectors.

\paragraph{Intended use.}
PDS is meant to help practitioners evaluate and compare defences against RAG
poisoning, not to serve as a guide to evading them. Where we describe a successful
evasion we pair it with the gap it exposes and the step that closes it.
