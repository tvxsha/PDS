\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:system}

The system scores a document with five signals. Four of them (detectors A to D)
are fused into one score; the fifth (detector E) is an external classifier that
we only compare against. Every signal returns a number, and a document is
flagged when its score exceeds a threshold fixed on training data
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}). Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the flow and
Table~\ref{tab:latency} gives what each signal costs; the costs were measured on
one laptop CPU (four threads, plugged in, 200 documents, three repeats).

\subsection{Detectors}
\paragraph{A: instruction pattern matching.}
A regular-expression detector scans the text for known override phrases
(variants of ``ignore previous instructions,'' fake system-authority tags such
as \texttt{[SYSTEM OVERRIDE]}, credential-exfiltration phrasing, and, after our
red-teaming, phrasing that exposes default or hard-coded credentials). Each
pattern $k$ carries a fixed weight $u_k\in[0,1]$ and the score is the largest
weight among the patterns that match,
\begin{equation}
    s_A(d)=\max_{k}\,u_k\,m_k(d),\qquad m_k(d)\in\{0,1\},
    \label{eq:pattern}
\end{equation}
where $m_k(d)=1$ if pattern $k$ matches $d$. It is effectively free (median
\resLatMedPat\,ms) but blind to any attack that does not use a known phrasing.

\paragraph{B: embedding anomaly.}
A sentence-embedding model $e(\cdot)$ (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) encodes the document
and a reference corpus $\mathcal{R}$ of trusted documents (the baseline role of
the split). A document far from every trusted example scores as more anomalous,
\begin{equation}
    s_B(d)=\operatorname{clip}\!\Big(\tfrac12\big[1-\max_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\cos\big(e(d),e(r)\big)\big],\,0,\,1\Big),
    \label{eq:embedding}
\end{equation}
with $\operatorname{clip}(x,0,1)=\min\{\max\{x,0\},1\}$. Median cost is
\resLatMedEmb\,ms.

\paragraph{C: perplexity.}
GPT-2 computes the perplexity of the first $N\le 1{,}024$ tokens $x_1,\dots,x_N$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{PPL}(d)=\exp\!\Big(-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{t=2}^{N}\log p\big(x_t\mid x_{<t}\big)\Big),
    \label{eq:ppl}
\end{equation}
and both very smooth and garbled text raise the score,
\begin{equation}
    s_C(d)=\begin{cases}
        \min\{1,\,(20-\mathrm{PPL})/20\}, & \mathrm{PPL}<20,\\[2pt]
        \min\{1,\,(\mathrm{PPL}-150)/150\}, & \mathrm{PPL}>150,\\[2pt]
        0, & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:pplscore}
\end{equation}
The bands 20 and 150 were set as rough defaults and not tuned. This is the most
expensive signal (median \resLatMedPpl\,ms, 95th percentile
\resLatPNinetyFivePpl\,ms).

\paragraph{D: sentence-level instruction classifier.}
The document is split into a set $U(d)$ of sentence-like units. A logistic
regression over word and bigram TF-IDF features and a few hand-written cues
(second-person address, modal verbs such as ``must'' or ``please'') maps every
unit to a probability, and the highest unit score is the document score, so a
short instruction hidden in a long document is not diluted,
\begin{equation}
    s_D(d)=\max_{u\in U(d)}\ \sigma\big(\mathbf{v}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\phi}(u)+c\big),\qquad
    \sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}.
    \label{eq:sentence}
\end{equation}
It is trained on the training role only, from document labels alone: every
sentence of a poisoned document is labelled poisoned and every sentence of a
clean document clean. Sentences that occur in both classes then cancel out and
the model learns the wording that appears only in attacks. Median cost is
\resLatMedSen\,ms.

\paragraph{E: published classifier.}
For comparison we run a public prompt-injection classifier
(\texttt{protectai/\allowbreak deberta-v3-base-\allowbreak prompt-injection-v2})~\cite{protectai2024deberta}.
It reads at most 512 tokens, so a document is cut into overlapping windows
$W(d)$ (at most eight) and the score is the margin of the most suspicious one,
\begin{equation}
    s_E(d)=\max_{w\in W(d)}\big[\ell_{\mathrm{inj}}(w)-\ell_{\mathrm{safe}}(w)\big],
    \label{eq:published}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ are the output logits of the injection and the other label. It is
not fitted on our data; the training role only fixes its cut-off. Median cost
is \resLatMedPub\,ms.

\subsection{Fusion}
Write $\mathbf{s}(d)=(s_A,s_B,s_C,s_D)$. The fused score is a logistic
regression,
\begin{equation}
    s(d)=\sigma\big(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{s}(d)+b\big),
    \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
fitted on the training role by minimising the class-balanced, $\ell_2$-penalised
log-loss
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \min_{\mathbf{w},b}\ \tfrac12\|\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+C\sum_{i}\omega_{y_i}\,\ell(y_i,s_i),\\
    \ell(y,s)=-y\log s-(1-y)\log(1-s),\qquad \omega_y=\frac{n}{2n_y},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:lr}
\end{equation}
with $C=1$ and $n_y$ the number of training documents of class $y$. The
sentence score that the regression sees in training is out-of-fold.

We compared three feature sets $\mathcal{F}$: A to C, A to D, and A to D plus E.
The rule for choosing among them was written before any validation number was
read. With $A(\mathcal{F})$ the mean out-of-fold training AUC of feature set
$\mathcal{F}$ over the three seeds,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    \mathcal{F}^{\star}=\arg\min_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{T}}\ \mathrm{cost}(\mathcal{F}),\\
    \mathcal{T}=\big\{\mathcal{F}:\ A(\mathcal{F})\ge\max\nolimits_{\mathcal{F}'}A(\mathcal{F}')-0.005\big\},
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:select}
\end{equation}
that is, sets within 0.005 of the best are tied and the one with fewer features
and cheaper signals wins. Adding the sentence classifier raised the training AUC
from 0.76 to 0.92; adding the published classifier on top changed nothing
(0.92), so A to D is the final feature set. We also report the naive average of
A to C and each signal alone.

\subsection{Thresholds}
For a false-alarm budget $\alpha\in\{5\%,1\%\}$, the threshold of every method
is the upper quantile of its out-of-fold scores $\tilde s$ on the training clean
documents $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}^{0}$,
\begin{equation}
    \tau_\alpha=Q_{1-\alpha}\big(\{\tilde s(d):\,d\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}^{0}\}\big),
    \qquad \text{flag } d \iff s(d)>\tau_\alpha ,
    \label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
applied unchanged to held-out documents (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}). Nothing is
tuned on validation or test.

\subsection{Cost-aware cascade}
Running all four signals on every document costs $\sum_i c_i$, where $c_i$ is the
median time of stage $i$, and perplexity alone is most of it. We therefore
measure two cascades whose stages are ordered by median cost (pattern, sentence
classifier, embedding, perplexity). In the \emph{OR cascade} (design A), stage
$i$ flags a document if $s_i(d)>\theta_i$ and otherwise passes it on. The budget
is split across stages, $\sum_i\alpha_i=\alpha$, each $\theta_i$ is the
$(1-\alpha_i)$ quantile of that stage's out-of-fold training clean scores, and
the split with the highest training recall is used. By the union bound the
false-alarm rate of the cascade on the training distribution is at most
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{FPR}\ \le\ \sum_i\alpha_i=\alpha .
    \label{eq:union}
\end{equation}
The rate realised on held-out documents can differ from this bound, and
Table~\ref{tab:cascade} reports it.
In the \emph{clear-early cascade} (design B) a stage may also allow a document at
once when $s_i(d)<\kappa_i$, where
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_i=Q_{q}\big(\{s_i(d):\,d\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}^{1}\}\big),\qquad q\in\{1\%,2\%,5\%\},
    \label{eq:clear}
\end{equation}
is the $q$-quantile of that stage's score among poisoned training documents, so
each stage clears at most about $q$ of the attacks. With $\rho_i$ the share of
documents that reach stage $i$ ($\rho_1=1$), the expected cost and the saving
against running everything are
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[L]=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\rho_i\,c_i,\qquad
    \text{saving}=1-\frac{\mathbb{E}[L]}{\sum_i c_i}.
    \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
Milliseconds in Table~\ref{tab:cascade} are such expectations, built from the
per-signal medians in Table~\ref{tab:latency}; they are not end-to-end
measurements, and the 95th percentile is much higher than the median for the
slower signals.
