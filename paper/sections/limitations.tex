\section{Limitations and Negative Results}
\label{sec:limitations}

Each limitation names the section, table or file that supports it.

\paragraph{Synthetic, English-language benchmarks.}
Most poisoned documents come from two benchmarks: BIPIA inserts a fixed attack
string into a host document, and PoisonedRAG passages are generated by a language
model (Table~\ref{tab:pool}). The in-house documents were written by the team. No
source is traffic from a deployed system, so the numbers describe benchmark
attacks, not attackers in the wild. A blind audit of the labels was planned but
the annotation was not completed (\texttt{docs/label\_audit.md}), so label noise
in the pooled sources is unmeasured.

\paragraph{Small clean samples.}
A test role holds $\resNNegA$ clean documents, so one false alarm moves the
false-alarm rate by about 0.6 percentage points, and a method with no false alarms
is only shown to be below roughly 2\% (rule of three, Eq.~\ref{eq:precision}).
The three seeds share most documents, so the range over seeds shows stability to
the split, not independent replication; the bootstrap intervals are within a seed.

\paragraph{One pooled threshold, known sources.}
The test role contains the same sources as the training role. Section~\ref{sec:loso}
shows what that hides: when a whole source is held out, the pooled threshold
flags most clean documents (BIPIA) or almost no poisoned ones (PoisonedRAG), and
Fig.~\ref{fig:source} shows unequal false-alarm rates across sources even within
the corpus. We did not implement per-source or conformal thresholds, which give a
guarantee under exchangeability; an empirical quantile of training scores gives
none, and the realised rate on held-out documents can differ from the budget
(Table~\ref{tab:headline}).

\paragraph{The strongest signal partly learned corpus style.}
Section~\ref{sec:learned} finds that a style-only control recovers at least 80\%
of the sentence classifier's PoisonedRAG recall in all three seeds. The check used
validation documents and one control, and no other poisoning generator was tried,
so how much of the signal survives a new generator is unknown.

\paragraph{Pretrained models and possible contamination.}
Model cards do not list training documents. The card of the sentence-embedding
model (\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}) names Natural Questions among its training data,
and the PoisonedRAG clean passages come from that corpus; the published classifier
was trained on public prompt-injection data without an item-level manifest.
Overlap with our sources therefore cannot be excluded
(\texttt{docs/contamination.md}, a review of documentation, not a test of the
weights).

\paragraph{Adversarial robustness is not measured.}
The only adversarial documents are ten written by the team
(Section~\ref{sec:devset}); the pattern that catches the last of them was written
after reading it, so it is not evidence of robustness. We did not test
obfuscation (homoglyphs, zero-width characters, encodings), paraphrase, position
or length sensitivity, a normalise-then-match defence, or an attacker who knows the
detectors and thresholds. A blind adversarial set was planned and not run, and
fifty further documents drafted by a teammate were not evaluated.

\paragraph{Planned analyses not done.}
We waived the boilerplate-subtraction experiment for the embedding signal;
domain-aware embedding variants were compared on training documents and the
original signal was kept because none beat it by the margin set in advance. We
did not evaluate a block, review or allow policy, nor conformal thresholds
(Section~\ref{sec:future}).

\paragraph{Cost and effect on answers.}
Latency was measured on one laptop CPU, with four threads, 200 documents and three
repeats (Table~\ref{tab:latency}); the cascade costs are expected values built from
these medians, not end-to-end timings. We did not measure whether removing flagged
documents improves the answers of the downstream model, which is the quantity a
deployer cares about.

\paragraph{Negative results.}
Four results did not hold up and we report them as such: the development-set
pipeline does not transfer to external sources (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}); the
pooled threshold does not transfer to a held-out source
(Section~\ref{sec:loso}); segment-level scoring did not help
(Section~\ref{sec:devset}); and the embedding signal flags every benign
out-of-domain document it was shown, so its in-domain accuracy overstates what it
measures.
